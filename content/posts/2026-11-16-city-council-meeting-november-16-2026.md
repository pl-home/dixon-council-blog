# Council Approves Oil Change Shop for Gateway Area, Begins Tax Levy Process

The Dixon City Council approved every item on its agenda at its most recent meeting. All five members voted yes on each roll call. The council made way for a quick-service oil change business in the Gateway development, began the annual property tax levy process, and accepted another donated vacant property for clearing. A resident used the public comment period to challenge the city on gaps in its own ethics ordinance.

## Oil change business coming to Gateway

The council held a public hearing and then approved an amendment to the city's annexation agreement with Dixon Land Group LLC, the developer behind the Gateway area that includes 7 Brew, Chipotle, and Jersey Mike's. The change allows an express oil change and lubrication business on the lot between 7 Brew and the car wash.

City Manager Danny Langloss said the original agreement was unclear on automotive uses. The city still does not want mechanic shops or body shops in the area, he said, but a quick-service oil change fits. Officials noted the amendment requires the business to be transactional, with no vehicles left overnight.

City Attorney Robert LeSage explained that annexation agreements can last up to 20 years. Any amendment requires a published notice, a public hearing, and approval by at least four of the five council members. No one from the public spoke at the hearing.

## Property tax levy placed on file

The council placed the proposed 2026 tax levy on file. A finance department staff member, identified in the recording as Ms. McCarter, presented it. The schedule ahead:

- **Nov. 24:** Hearing notice published in the newspaper
- **Dec. 7:** Public hearing at the council meeting
- **Dec. 21:** Council vote on the levy
- **Dec. 22:** Levy filed with the county

Staff presented a proposed levy of $5,577,876. Because of tax caps, the city expects to collect $5,459,243, about $144,012 more than it received for 2025.

Staff said the city's tax rate is expected to drop from 2.2578 to 2.1687. For a $100,000 home whose assessment does not change, that works out to about $30 less on the city's share of the bill. Staff cautioned that homeowners whose assessments rise may not see that savings.

## Other actions

- **Donated property:** The council accepted the donation of a vacant property at 805 W. Sixth St. A council action form had listed the address incorrectly, but the ordinance carried the correct one. Building Official Tim Shipman said this continues the city's effort to take over long-abandoned properties, as it has done on Fourth and Seventh streets. The city expects the Strong Communities Grant to pay for clearing the lot.
- **Library grant:** The council amended the FY 2026-27 budget to account for a state technology grant received by the Dixon Public Library.
- **EPA grant requirement:** The council adopted a civil rights non-discrimination and grievance procedure, which the EPA requires for a grant the city has received. LeSage said the procedure covers only how the grant money is spent, such as a complaint that a funded facility is not handicapped accessible. It does not replace employee policies.
- **Routine business:** The council approved the Nov. 2 minutes, the October year-to-date financials, and the labor and outside claims. Labor and outside claims totaled $1,014,527.18. Mayor Glen Hughes noted the largest single item was $537,180 for the ongoing wastewater treatment plant improvement project.

## Resident raises ethics, meeting-order concerns

Resident Brin Slager (spelling of name not confirmed) made two points about the city's own code:

1. **Meeting order:** The order of business in city code places public comment differently than current agendas do. She argued public comment belongs near the end of meetings.
2. **Ethics officials:** City code requires the mayor, with council consent, to designate an ethics advisor and appoint a three-member ethics commission. She said the mayor told her by email that the city has had neither in more than 20 years.

Citing the Rita Crundwell embezzlement, she urged the council to adopt an ethics code stricter than state minimums and suggested using the Illinois City/County Management Association's code as a model.

The council did not respond during public comment. In his report, Langloss said his contract requires him to belong to the ICMA and ILCMA and to follow their ethics code, which he teaches to other Illinois city managers. He acknowledged that gaps in older ordinances matter. No action on the ethics advisor or commission was scheduled.

## Cross-country champions recognized

The council recognized the Dixon High School boys cross-country team, which won its second straight Class 2A state title and the third in school history. Coach Thorpe said the 13-member team also placed fourth of 31 teams at last weekend's Nike regional meet, which covers five states. The team is hoping for an at-large invitation to nationals in Portland. One senior has signed to run at the University of Evansville.

## Reports and comments

- **Human resources:** HR staff said the new UKG payroll and personnel software is about 75% implemented. Next steps include tracking employee licenses and certifications, such as CDLs, by the end of December. The employee United Way campaign raised roughly $8,000.
- **Marketing:** The city's social media accounts have about 11,800 followers. Staff are also working to make the city website ADA-accessible.
- **Holiday decorations:** Langloss said Street Department crews now put up holiday decorations in about two and a half days, down from more than two weeks.
- **Shop local:** Mayor Hughes urged residents to shop locally this holiday season. Councilman Mary Oros noted that sales tax makes up roughly 45% of the city budget and suggested reviving a COVID-era shop-local social media campaign.
- **Dixon Theater sign:** Councilman Mike Venier thanked the Paul Rowe family for funding the new electronic billboard on the theater's south wall.
- **New department heads:** Councilman Chris Bishop credited employees who have recently moved into new department head roles.
- Councilman Dennis Considine had no comments.

**Next meeting:** Dec. 7, including the public hearing on the tax levy.

### Tax levy

Placing the levy on file before a later vote has been the council's practice for years. On Nov. 4, 2013, Finance Director Paula Meyer told the council the levy was being placed on file that night and would be approved at the first meeting in December; she noted that year's levy was close to the prior year's but included a 10% increase in police and fire pensions. On Nov. 1, 2021, Finance Director Fredericks presented the levy and its timeline, and the council placed it on file on a motion by Councilman Bishop, seconded by Councilman Considine.
