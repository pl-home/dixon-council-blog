# Dixon City Council Meeting Summary

**Regular Session — Monday, September 21, 2026**

**All council members present:** Mayor Glen Hughes, Councilmen Chris Bishop, Dennis Considine, Mary Oros, and Mike Venier.

---

## Resident Pleads for Help with Problem Property

The most striking moment of the meeting came during public comment, when longtime West 2nd Street resident Jonathan Scott Pottinger delivered a detailed account of nearly five years of trouble emanating from a neighboring property at 624 West 2nd Street. Armed with 240 pages of police reports documenting more than 50 incidents over 18 months, Pottinger described ongoing illegal burning of scrap materials, domestic violence, drug activity, and constant disturbances at all hours.

Pottinger said the burning has affected his son's asthma and his wife's migraines. He noted that of 11 police responses to illegal fires, citations were issued only five times, and the Dixon Fire Department had to extinguish unattended fires on two occasions. Of 20 police responses to screaming and fighting, only three resulted in citations. He said the residents of the problem property have learned to simply not answer the door when officers arrive, at which point police leave.

He played an audio recording from his bedroom to illustrate the noise, with profanity beeped out. He asked the council and police department to pursue declaring the property a nuisance and to impose meaningful consequences.

Councilman Venier called the presentation "very thorough" and said no one should have to live under those conditions. City Manager Danny Langloss Jr. said the city would coordinate with the police chief and building department to explore additional actions. Pottinger said he'd had a productive conversation with the police chief but noted that previous efforts through police, the building department, and even direct contact with the property's owners and tenants had produced no lasting results.

## Flock Camera Update

Resident John Slager raised the topic of Flock surveillance cameras, noting that Dixon has cameras controlled by Lee County and that the city of Sterling announced the same day it was removing all its Flock cameras, weighing their benefit against concerns they create. He encouraged the council to review Sterling's approach.

## Building Department Activity Report

Building Official Tim Shipman reported that through the first eight months of 2026, his department issued 518 building permits representing $20.6 million in construction work and about $47,000 in permit fees. The department received 224 complaints ranging from tall grass to junk vehicles, issued 40 notices to mow (the worst year in recent memory, Shipman said — "People just won't mow their yard"), and processed 75 cases through administrative hearings. He also reported 104 home-sale electrical verifications and 189 inspections.

Shipman said the department is rolling out new iWork permitting software, expected to go live in early October, and plans to update the city's building codes from the 2015 international code and review its fee schedule this winter.

## E-Bike Regulations Coming

City Manager Langloss reported that a city team including police, public works, council members, and the city attorney — along with the Park District — met to discuss new state e-bike legislation taking effect January 1, 2027. Rather than create local rules that would conflict with the incoming state law, the city will focus on public education. The police department will lead a marketing campaign through social media and press releases, and education sessions are planned for local schools.

Langloss urged parents considering e-bikes, e-scooters, or e-skateboards for their children to familiarize themselves with the new law, which includes age restrictions varying by device type.

## ITEP South Path — Public Meeting Wednesday

Public Works Director Matt Heckman announced a public open house for Wednesday, September 23, from 4 to 6 p.m. at City Hall for the proposed ITEP South shared-use path project. The path would run from Taylor's Court in Pirups Landing south through Bloody Gulch Park to Fulfs Lane in the Gateway Development, with lighting included. Residents can review the project scope and ask questions.

## Railroad Property Purchase for ITEP East Path

The council unanimously approved two ordinances authorizing agreements to purchase roughly one acre of former railroad property along East River Road from Union Pacific and Illinois Central Railroad, at $60,000 each ($120,000 total). The property is needed to extend the existing river path eastward as part of the ITEP East project, for which the city was previously awarded $2.25 million in grant funding. Heckman noted that approval begins the acquisition process but does not finalize the purchase — title work, surveys, and due diligence remain.

## ITEP South Grant Support Resolution

The council approved a resolution formally supporting the city's ITEP South grant application to IDOT, due October 5. Heckman clarified a previous clerical error: the city is seeking $3.11 million in grant funding, not $4.1 million. The total project cost is estimated at $4.1 million with a local match of $990,000 (about 24% due to the 50-50 cost split on pathway lighting). Cost estimates are based on 2029 construction projections.

## Appointments

The council unanimously approved three appointments:
- **Drew Raleigh**, an investment professional, to the Board of Trustees of the Fire Pension Fund (term ending April 30, 2027).
- **Greg Gates and Theresa Tucker**, reappointed to the Plan Commission (terms ending August 31, 2030).
- **Michelle Hay**, appointed to the Plan Commission (term ending August 31, 2030).

## Council Comments

Councilman Considine shared his experience judging the Dixon Theatre's student talent show fundraiser, which ran six hours instead of the anticipated two to three. He praised the young performers and the community turnout.

Councilwoman Oros reported on attending the Illinois Municipal League Conference in Chicago, highlighting a housing forum she called particularly impactful, with panelists from Rockford sharing strategies for addressing blight and housing challenges.

Councilman Venier recognized Major General Henry S. Dixon, a Dixon High School graduate and descendant of the city's founder, who was inducted into the University of Illinois ROTC Hall of Fame in conjunction with his retirement after 39 years in the Illinois Army National Guard, including tours in Iraq and Afghanistan.

Venier also noted that weekend rain forced an outdoor festival to move indoors to Madwater, and praised two tribute band performances at the Dixon Theatre while encouraging better community attendance to sustain quality entertainment.

---

*The meeting adjourned with all agenda items completed. All votes were unanimous.*
