# Dixon Council Approves Records Software Upgrade and Habitat Lot Sale; Neighbor Says Illegal Burning Continues

**DIXON —** The Dixon City Council met Monday, Nov. 2, with all members present: Mayor Glen Hughes and Councilmen Chris Bishop, Dennis Considine, Mary Oros and Mike Venier. The council approved a records-software upgrade, the sale of a city lot to Habitat for Humanity, and a residential rezoning. It then went into closed session to discuss pending litigation.

## Residents say open burning continues on West 2nd Street

Jonathan Pottinger returned to the council about problems at a neighboring property on West 2nd Street. He thanked the council for revising the city's open-burning ordinance within about two weeks of his first appearance six weeks ago. He said the problems eased for about three weeks and then came back.

Pottinger described:
- **Fires:** Nearly daily fires burning construction debris and other materials.
- **Noise:** Yelling that has woken his family between 2 and 4 a.m.
- **The latest incident:** On Sunday, he said, an unattended fire filled his home with smoke for about 14 hours.

According to Pottinger, residents of the property told police the fire was for cooking. That is the only exception allowed under the ordinance, but he said no food or cooking equipment was present. He said one responding officer told him nothing more would be done, that the city "had done a lot for me lately," and that he could be cited if he kept handling matters himself. Earlier that day he had used a megaphone and sprayed the fire with a garden hose from his side of the fence. He said Sgt. Pratt later arrived, put out the fire and calmed the situation. Pottinger praised the other officers who have responded.

City Manager Danny Langloss Jr. said he had spoken with Police Chief Ryan Bivins about the officer's conduct. He said the department will review body camera footage and Bivins will follow up.

Adam Pottinger added that the fires burn garbage, bags and chemically treated wood. He said the smoke reaches neighbors with young children and the nearby viaduct walking path. He also said a fuel can and other flammable materials sit within about 20 feet of the fire pit.

Jonathan Slager spoke about civility and respectful disagreement. He praised Chief Bivins for a recent newspaper comment acknowledging that the department can do better. He also thanked Fire Chief Ryan Buskohl's department for its response when his daughters have had seizures.

## Records system moves to the cloud

The council approved moving the city's Laserfish document-management system to a cloud-based "municipality" license. The vote was 5-0.

IT Manager Allen Philhower gave these reasons for the change:
- **Support deadlines:** Support for the city's current version ends Dec. 30 of this year. Laserfish will end all support for in-house systems in December 2030.
- **Unexpected licensing requirement:** The city had planned the move since August 2025. It later learned that the security-compliance add-on it needs for certain protected data requires the more expensive municipality license.
- **Cost:** The first-year cost is $30,714.05, and the three-year total is about $97,900. Philhower estimated that staying with the in-house system would cost at least $28,000 this year, without the added features of the cloud version.

The software budget line has about $180,000 available. Philhower said the purchase runs roughly $20,000 over what was planned for this item. Savings on antivirus software ($4,500), phone maintenance ($4,000) and e-ticketing ($2,500) offset part of that, and any remaining gap can be covered by moving money within the IT budget.

## Lead service line inventory

The council voted 5-0 to approve a professional services agreement with Fehr Graham, not to exceed $57,500. The firm will hire a contractor to dig at roughly 307 water service lines to finish the lead service line inventory the EPA requires. A $40,000 grant covers most of the cost, and the rest comes from the city's capital funds.

## Lot sold to Habitat for Humanity

The council voted 5-0 to sell 502 W. Seventh St. to Dixon Habitat for Humanity, which plans to build its 2027 house there. The lot was cleared when the city demolished five houses last year. Because the property lies in a TIF district, the city had to advertise it for other proposals. None were received, so a related agenda item was dropped. The sale price was not discussed at the meeting.

## Independence Court rezoning

The council voted 5-0 to rezone Lot 15 of Independence Court Phase 2 from general business (B2) to two-family residential (R2). Andrew Robertson of Robertson Construction and Development plans to build a duplex there, similar to one he just finished on the neighboring lot. The Plan Commission had recommended approval.

## Budget amendment and library grant

- **Ambulance billing:** The council voted 5-0 to amend the budget to show the 4 percent fee charged by Paramedic Billing Services, the company that now bills for EMS transports. The fee is deducted before revenue reaches the city. Fire Chief Buskohl said listing it as an expense will make the true cost visible and improve auditing.
- **Library grant:** The council voted 5–0 to authorize City Manager Danny Langloss to sign the agreement and related documents for a $210,000 USDA Rural Housing Service community facilities grant for the Dixon Public Library renovation. A new signer was needed after retirements, and officials said having the city manager sign provides consistency on long-term documents.

## Routine business

The council approved each of the following 5–0 by roll call:
- **Minutes:** Approved for the Oct. 19 regular meeting and the Oct. 26 special and executive sessions.
- **Bills:** Approved $1,862,912.35 in labor and outside claims. The largest items were bond principal and interest payments of $691,202 for the police pension fund and $466,589 for the firefighters' pension fund.
- **Financials:** Accepted the year-to-date financial summary through September.

## Reports and announcements

- **Sidewalk savings:** Langloss said the street and water departments, under the Public Works Director, handled a long stretch of sidewalk on Van Buren and other locations in-house instead of contracting it out. He said this saved about $100,000, roughly enough to pave two and a half blocks of road. The city's sidewalk program is underway; homeowners pay for the concrete and city crews do the work.
- **Halloween:** Langloss thanked police and fire personnel who worked Halloween. He said he heard of no major incidents.
- **Elections and Veterans Day:** Mayor Hughes reminded residents that Tuesday was Election Day and that Veterans Day falls on Wednesday, Nov. 11.
- **Sports:** Hughes congratulated the cross country teams, which are headed to state; the boys team took first at sectionals. The Dukes football team won its second-round playoff game and plays Coal City on Saturday. Venier also congratulated the team.
- **Government Day:** Oros thanked Bishop for inviting council members to Government Day at Reagan Middle School. She said several students asked to attend a council meeting to offer ideas on housing.

The council then voted 5-0 to go into executive session to discuss pending litigation. No action was reported afterward.
