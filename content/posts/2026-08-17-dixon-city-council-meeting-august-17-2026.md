# Dixon City Council Meeting Summary
## Regular Session — August 17, 2026

**All council members present:** Mayor Glen Hughes, Councilmen Chris Bishop, Dennis Considine, Mary Oros, and Mike Venier. Invocation by Father John Evans of St. Patrick's Catholic Church.

---

### Public Comment

**Jonathan Slager** asked the council about the status of Flock license plate reader cameras in Dixon. City Manager Danny Langloss Jr. confirmed the city does not currently have any Flock cameras and has no plans to bring the issue forward. The council turned down a Flock camera proposal three years ago.

---

### Financial Business

The council approved claims totaling $290,400 for August 3 and $1,527,063 for August 17. City Manager Langloss noted that roughly $1.1 million of the larger amount was for wastewater treatment plant improvements. Year-to-date financials through July 2026 were also approved.

---

### Department Reports

**Police Chief Ryan Bivins** covered three topics:

- **E-bikes:** The department has launched an education campaign, with officers on directed patrols making contact with juvenile riders and their parents. Bivins noted that the state e-bike legislation has not yet been signed by the governor, so the department is coordinating with City Attorney Robert LeSage on what can currently be enforced. The department is also looking at adopting Heritage Crossing pathway ordinances and plans to offer an e-bike safety class.

- **New police app:** The department has launched a mobile app allowing residents to submit tips, request extra patrols, file complaints, and receive emergency weather notifications. Bivins pushed back on social media criticism that the app is a data-collection tool, calling that "the furthest thing from the truth."

- **Staffing:** The department is approaching its authorized strength of 31 officers, with one just graduating from the academy. Once fully staffed, Officer Lincoln Sharp will move into a dedicated community engagement role. Upcoming events include a Quarter Mania fundraiser for a second police canine, a Clothes for Kids school supply event, and a Cops and Bobbers fishing event September 19 at Page Park.

**Street Department Manager Tyler Venier** reported on Petunia Fest logistics, praising streets foreman Matt Kirchner and properties foreman Kevin Spratt by name for their work during the festival. He also credited Deputy Chief Chris Scott for developing the event's operational safety plan in his first year leading that effort.

On infrastructure, Venier reported completion of 310 feet of curb/gutter and 150+ feet of sidewalk at the Loveland Community Building, and near-completion of 575 feet of sidewalk on South Peoria Avenue. The sidewalk program for residents who signed up will begin within weeks. Fall plans include street sign work and aggressive tree trimming along roadways ahead of plow season. He noted August had already seen over eight inches of rain.

---

### City Manager's Report

**Danny Langloss Jr.** highlighted:

- **Golf cart rides for seniors and residents with disabilities** along the Heritage Crossing bike path drew a crowd that kept six golf carts running for four straight hours. The event was sponsored by the Dixon Park District. Langloss dismissed online criticism about overtime costs, saying participation was entirely voluntary.

- **Congressman Hood** visited Dixon and toured the Dixon Family YMCA. The city has been selected for a **$1 million federal grant** for major YMCA renovations, though funding must still be included in the federal budget, likely not until early 2027. Langloss also noted the YMCA's childcare facility now has 135 children enrolled, "well above the breakeven point."

- The **Bloody Gulch Park** retention pond project, also connected to federal support, will begin soon.

---

### Council Comments

Mayor Hughes praised Venetian Night as a successful event and congratulated Councilman Mary Oros on receiving a **"Woman of Impact" award** from the Illinois Bankers Association. Other council members echoed the congratulations. Oros described witnessing officers conducting e-bike education on the bike path during the golf cart rides, calling the interactions effective and professional.

---

### Ordinances Approved

- **Liquor code amendment** for an additional Dixon City Market pop-up on August 26, requested by the Dixon Chamber of Commerce and Dixon Main Street due to weather cancellations earlier in the season. Passed unanimously.

- **Liquor code amendment** for a **Dog Day Afternoon** event organized by Virology Pet Spa, to be held in the Bean Blossom area with music, vendors, pet activities, and fundraising for Granny Rose rescue. Passed unanimously.

- **Disposal of surplus city computing equipment.** IT Manager Allen Philhower said most items will be recycled, with a few available for employee purchase at buyback rates. Passed unanimously.

---

### Budget Amendments Approved

- **EV Fund reallocation:** Fire Chief Ryan Buskohl explained that $36,000 budgeted for portable radio replacements is being redirected to cover radio infrastructure repairs delayed from last fiscal year due to parts shortages. No new money required; portable radio purchases deferred to next year. Passed unanimously.

- **Library grant expense increase:** Finance Director Becky Leslie explained the Dixon Public Library received additional per capita grant funds, requiring a budget adjustment to allow spending on materials and books. Passed unanimously.

---

### Purchases and Contracts Approved

- **16 new street lights** from Steiner Electric for $87,328 — under the $130,000 budget by nearly $43,000. The lights will replace aging fixtures along South Galena Avenue between Second and Third Streets and near the YMCA. Installation will be done by city staff. Tyler Venier said the department will attempt to sandblast and refurbish some old poles but most are too corroded. Passed unanimously.

- **Engineering agreement amendment** with Willett Hofmann & Associates for Well No. 9 water tower painting, covering additional construction engineering time required during an extended vetting process. Passed unanimously.

---

The meeting adjourned after all agenda items were completed.
