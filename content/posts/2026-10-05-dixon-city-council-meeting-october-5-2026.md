# Dixon City Council Meeting Summary — October 5, 2026

The Dixon City Council held a brief regular session Monday evening, with all four council members and Mayor Glen Hughes present. City Manager Danny Langloss Jr. was absent, attending a conference along with several department heads.

## Domestic Violence Awareness Month

Mayor Hughes opened with a proclamation declaring October 2026 as Domestic Violence Awareness Month in Dixon, urging citizens to support efforts to prevent domestic violence and assist survivors. A representative from the YWCA accepted the proclamation.

## Claims Approved

The council unanimously approved total labor and outside claims of approximately $1.51 million. The largest single item was a payment on the wastewater treatment plant improvement project; the exact dollar amount of that line item was not clearly captured in the audio.

## Sister City Visit to Germany

Mayor Hughes reported on his recent trip to Herzberg, Germany, Dixon's sister city since 1999. He described the relationship as strong, noting roughly seven visits in each direction over the years. A highlight was the creation of a mural — painted in just two days — that was unveiled behind Herzberg's community building. Hughes emphasized that the Sister Cities organization handles most travel costs through its own fundraising, with the city contributing about $6,000 for the most recent incoming visit.

## Council Member Reports

**Councilman Chris Bishop** pointed residents to a new mural being painted on the Water Department building. The artist, Nora, was reportedly near completion, with a dedication planned for mid-October.

**Councilwoman Mary Oros** recognized Justin Bergman, director of the Small Business Development Center at Sauk Valley Community College, who received the Illinois State Star award for his leadership. Oros praised the center as a free resource for local entrepreneurs and existing business owners.

**Councilman Mike Venier** covered several items:
- He congratulated Bunnell Industries on a ribbon cutting for a new 100,000-square-foot manufacturing facility, noting the company's 66 years in business and 109 employees.
- He announced a golf cart tour of the city's new trail for seniors and those unable to walk it, scheduled for October 13 from 9 a.m. to 1 p.m., starting near the Emma Hubs Tennis Courts.
- He mentioned the mural on the Water Department building features "Visions of the Rock," depicting scenes indigenous to the Rock River.
- He reminded residents of the Ultimate Tailgate event (Bears-Packers, ribs and chili cook-off) on Sunday in the Bean Blossom parking lot, and Scarecrow Fest on October 17.
- He noted Dixon's 200th anniversary is approaching in a couple of years and said the municipal band is working on a commemorative song.

## Chronic Nuisance Property Ordinance Overhauled

In the most substantive action of the evening, the council unanimously approved a major rewrite of the city's chronic nuisance property ordinance — the first update in roughly 30 years.

City Attorney Robert LeSage III explained that Police Chief Ryan Bivins wanted a more practical enforcement tool, particularly in light of concerns raised at the previous meeting. Key changes include:

- **Expanded qualifying violations** from 5 to 19, adding modern criminal code provisions and serious property maintenance violations.
- **Clarified repeat-offense rules**: A single type of violation occurring three separate times in distinct situations can now qualify a property as a chronic nuisance — previously the language seemed to require three *different* types of violations.
- **Extended the look-back period** from 90 to 120 days.
- **Improved due process protections**, including better notice requirements and opportunities for property owners to enter settlement agreements or abatement plans.
- **Broadened judicial remedies** beyond the old mandatory 30-day shuttering, giving judges more flexible options depending on the severity of the situation.

LeSage said new enforcement forms still need to be developed, so implementation will take some time. He noted the ordinance was reviewed by multiple officials including the city manager, police chief, Deputy Chief Aaron Simonton, and the mayor.

## Other Actions

- **Liquor code amendment**: Unanimously approved to allow alcohol in the restricted Bean Blossom parking lot area for the Ultimate Tailgate event, a recurring annual accommodation for the Dixon Chamber of Commerce.
- **Zoning referral**: The council unanimously referred a petition for a zoning amendment on behalf of Robertson Construction for Lot 15 of Liberty Court to the Plan Commission for review.

The meeting adjourned after roughly 25 minutes with no public comment.
