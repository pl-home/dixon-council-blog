# Dixon City Council Meeting Summary – October 19, 2026

## World Polio Day Proclamation

Mayor Glen Hughes opened the meeting by reading a proclamation declaring October 24, 2026, as World Polio Day in Dixon, recognizing Rotary International's decades-long effort to eradicate polio worldwide. Rotary members were present to receive the proclamation.

## City Audit Gets Clean Opinion

Bradley Johnson of the accounting firm Sikich presented the results of the city's fiscal year 2025-2026 audit. The city received a clean, unmodified opinion — the highest level of assurance an auditor can give. Overall net position increased by roughly $9.5 million, and the city's Illinois Municipal Retirement Fund is 112.45% funded, meaning it is well ahead of its pension obligations.

One significant deficiency was noted, though it was not related to grant compliance or misuse of funds. Rather, it involved the timing of how grant revenues and receivables were recorded at year-end. Finance Director Becky Leslie confirmed it was essentially a paperwork-timing issue — revenues were booked before final documentation was in hand. Management is tightening procedures. Council unanimously approved the audit.

## Public Comment: Resident Raises Police Accountability Concerns

Jonathan Slager, a 43-year Dixon resident, used the public comment period to raise concerns about police accountability. He referenced a previous resident's complaint about a problem property on 2nd Street that had generated roughly 20 calls but only three arrests. He also alleged that a police officer had been observed running stop signs in a squad car, including near Washington School, and that his attempt to report it was handled casually.

Slager also said he had been unable to get meetings with all council members, claiming Councilman Bishop told him he would only speak with Slager if Slager became mayor.

The comments drew a pointed response from Councilman Dennis Considine, who called the public criticism of officers and council members "despicable behavior" and said such matters should be handled through internal procedures rather than aired at a public meeting. Considine praised both the police and fire departments in strong terms, drawing on his experience since joining the council in 2011.

## Public Works Update

Public Works Director Matt Heckman delivered a progress report on several projects:

- **Street resurfacing** is underway, with paving roughly 15-20% complete. City crews handled extensive concrete work in-house — more than 1,000 feet of sidewalk and 21 driveway approaches — saving the city an estimated $100,000. Heckman praised the street and water crews by name for stepping up on short notice.
- **2nd Street Stone Arch rehabilitation** has been paused until spring due to drainage problems discovered during construction. Engineers are designing a permanent fix.
- **Fargo Creek culvert work**: The council later approved a $77,500 engineering agreement with Fehr Graham to design replacement of two culverts on Monroe Avenue and rehabilitation of one on Madison Avenue. The total project budget is $1.75 million, and staff expects to come in under that figure. Bidding is targeted for January.
- **Dimco properties and Moxie building demolition**: Specs are complete and awaiting final EPA guidance. The city hopes to bid the demolition in January and begin work this winter.

## Fire Department Report

Fire Chief Ryan Buskohl reported on a busy fall season:

- **HyperReach**, a mass notification system, is now active. Residents who sign up can receive emergency alerts — storm warnings, road closures, evacuations — by phone call or text. The chief encouraged residents to opt in via the fire department's Facebook page.
- The department has also applied for **iPAWS**, a FEMA platform that would allow the city to send Amber Alert-style notifications to every cell phone in a targeted geographic area during emergencies.
- October being Fire Prevention Month, the department conducted school fire drills, safety presentations to third graders, touch-a-truck events, and participated in a career fair at Sauk Valley Community College attended by some 1,600 eighth graders.
- Several firefighters have completed or are pursuing advanced degrees and certifications. Two firefighters recently finished paramedic training, which will make the department fully licensed with paramedics. Deputy Chief Adam Arnold is completing a year-long chief fire officer program.
- The department's transition to third-party EMS billing has gone smoothly, and a kitchen/day room expansion project at the station is complete — including a new table to supplement one that's reportedly over 100 years old.
- The apprentice program is running with four participants, up from the previous maximum of three.

## Council Member Comments

**Councilman Chris Bishop** praised Discover Dixon for the Ultimate Tailgate and Scarecrow Festival events and thanked city crews and police for event support. He also highlighted the police department's delivery of ALICE (active shooter) training at Reagan Middle School, noting Dixon's own experience with a school shooting.

**Councilwoman Mary Oros** echoed the praise for city crews who volunteer personal time to set up events and for law enforcement officers who spent their Saturday engaging with the community at Scarecrow Fest.

**Councilman Mike Venier** reported Scarecrow Fest drew an estimated 8,000 to 9,000 visitors downtown. He highlighted a new mural by local artist Nora Ruder — depicting local plant and animal species — painted on the city water department building through Discover Dixon's Coalition of the Arts. He also reminded residents of a "Tour the Trail" event for seniors on Friday, featuring golf cart rides along the trail, with coffee and refreshments provided by Sauk Valley Bank and golf carts sponsored by the Dixon Park District.

## Votes and Approvals

All items passed, though the roll-call votes for some items were not clearly captured in the audio:

- **Total labor and outside claims** of $612,875.73, including an IEPA loan payment and well painting costs.
- **Year-to-date financials** through August 2026.
- **Three budget amendments** involving internal fund shifts for police promotional exams ($12,250 total), police office supplies, and insurance line items — all involving no new money.
- **Reappointment** of four members and **appointment** of Al Wyckoff to the Veterans Memorial Park Commission.
- **Airport hangar rehabilitation** awarded to Boss Roofing and Siding Experts for $27,737.67 — well under the $40,000 budget. A second bid came in at $79,800.
- **Insurance min-max premium agreement** with the Illinois Municipal League Risk Management Association, locking in a minimum premium of $370,363 versus a traditional premium that would have been roughly $473,000. The city has saved approximately $450,000 over seven years through this approach.
- **Wastewater treatment plant storage building** awarded to NICAM Construction for $2,295,000. The project was budgeted at $2 million; the overage will be covered from existing capital fund balances. Councilman Considine, who has been waiting on a similar cemetery building, made sure to note his concern about prioritization but was assured the cemetery project remains on track.

The meeting adjourned after completing all agenda items.
